**VLC media player** is the rare program that became a default answer: a file will not open — you install VLC. Free, open source, without ads or premium plans, it has been downloaded several billion times. Here is what is behind that success.

## A student project that got out of hand

VLC started in 1996 as a network project at a Paris engineering school. In 2001 the VideoLAN non-profit released it to the public, and the famous traffic-cone logo came from a student joke: the founders collected real traffic cones and wore them at parties. More than twenty years later the same non-profit still develops the player, lives on donations and keeps the current 3.0 line updated with fresh codec support.

## What makes it special

- **Plays everything out of the box.** Decoders for virtually every video and audio format are built in — no codec packs to hunt for
- **Network streams.** IPTV, internet radio, HTTP and RTSP links open right in the player
- **Conversion and recording.** VLC converts files between formats and can even record your screen
- **Subtitles.** Built-in sync adjustment, plus the VLSub extension that downloads subtitles for a movie in one click
- **Control over playback.** Equalizer, video filters, speed control, A-B loop, bookmarks
- **Everywhere.** Windows, macOS, Linux, Android and iOS versions share the same no-nonsense approach

## Where it falls short

Honesty matters more than hype, so:

- The interface looks and feels dated compared with modern players
- Blu-ray support is limited, and DRM-protected streaming services like Netflix will not play — that is the price of staying DRM-free
- The redesigned VLC 4 with a proper media library has been in development for years

## Verdict

For playing video and audio VLC is still the safest choice there is: it runs on any system, eats any file and never asks for money. If you want a full media center instead, look at **Kodi**, and for streaming your own library at home — **Jellyfin**. But as a plain player, VLC has no real rival.
